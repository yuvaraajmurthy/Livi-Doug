■ Challenge Summary

ScarletWell can already describe its own program's data, but has no way
to see how that connects to what other units, such as Recreation or
Student Health, are doing, or whether any of it is closing the gaps
students report. This challenge asks teams to build a real-time,
university-wide dashboard that gives counseling staff, wellness program
leads, and administrators a single view of student wellness trends, so
they can see where students are struggling, where current resources are
and aren't reaching them, and where to prioritize outreach and staffing
next.

■ Dataset

De-identified Wellness Inventory results and related demographic and
behavioral health data across New Brunswick, Newark, and Camden; the
Rutgers academic calendar (midterms, finals, add/drop periods) to test
for correlation with wellness dips; publicly available local, national,
and public health event data (e.g., seasonal illness spikes) for the
same purpose; existing ScarletWell dashboard examples as a starting
reference point for visualization style and scope.

■ Your Challenge

Design an AI tool that can:

• Aggregate de-identified wellness data across campuses into a single,
real-time view for staff.
• Visualize wellness trends by dimension, campus/location, and
population (e.g., undergraduate vs. graduate, by school or program) over
time.
• Overlay wellness trends against known stressor periods, such as
academic calendar events and relevant local, national, or public health
events, to help identify when and where dips occur and whether they
correlate with an identifiable cause.
• Help staff spot disconnects between where resources exist and where
student wellness data suggests they are needed, to inform outreach and
resource allocation decisions.

■ Suggested Approaches

• Standard time-series and comparative visualizations (trend lines, heat
maps by campus/dimension) paired with simple anomaly or dip detection.
• An LLM-assisted natural-language query layer that lets staff ask
questions of the dashboard directly (e.g., "where did financial wellness
scores drop last month?").
• A modular data model that can later incorporate additional sources,
such as Recreation or Student Health program data, without a redesign.
• A clear, non-clinical presentation style suited to program leads and
administrators rather than a research audience.

■ Deliverables

A working prototype dashboard that demonstrates: at least one
interactive visualization built on sample de-identified wellness data; a
trend or dip identified in that data that the team ties to a plausible
calendar event or stressor period; and a short team walkthrough
explaining how a counselor or administrator would use the dashboard to
make a real decision about outreach or resources.
